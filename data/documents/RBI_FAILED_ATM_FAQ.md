# Failed ATM transactions — RBI FAQ summary for retrieval

**Document status:** This is a short paraphrased retrieval summary, not the verbatim text of an RBI publication. Before using it for any customer-facing or operational decision, open the official RBI links and confirm the rule is still current and applies to the transaction in question.

## When the account is debited but the ATM does not dispense cash

The RBI FAQ on failed ATM transactions says the bank should re-credit the account within a maximum of **T + 5 calendar days**, where **T** is the date of the transaction. It says compensation of **₹100 per day** is payable for delay beyond five calendar days, with compensation credited to the customer's account without a separate claim. This applies to the relevant failed ATM transaction category; it is not a generic rule for every ATM charge or card issue.

## Complaint and escalation

The FAQ recommends lodging a complaint with the card-issuing bank or ATM-owner bank at the earliest. If the customer is not satisfied with the bank's response, or does not receive a reply within 30 days, the FAQ points to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme as a further route. Verify current RBI escalation instructions and eligibility before advising an individual customer.

## How ATMResolve should use this document

- Retrieve this source when a complaint says the account was debited but cash was not dispensed, or that a failed transaction's reversal is delayed.
- Verify the transaction date, debit, cash-dispense outcome, reversal status and any ATM event evidence before drafting a recommendation.
- Keep policy text separate from observed facts. Do not infer the transaction result from the complaint category alone.
- Do not decide entitlement, promise compensation, submit an actual complaint, or initiate a payment automatically. Every demo case requires human review.
- Do not apply the T+5/₹100 rule to ATM annual maintenance charges, card-renewal fees, routine withdrawal fees, or unrelated transactions merely because money was deducted.
